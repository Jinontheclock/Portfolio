    Get involved

    TinyPaws has no shelter building — just people. Fosters, volunteers, and donors are the whole operation.

    Foster a litter

    Fostering means giving kittens a spare room and a routine for two to eight weeks while they grow big enough for adoption. We cover food, litter, supplies, and every vet visit — you supply the safe space and the socializing.

    • All supplies and vet costs covered
    • A mentor foster on call for every placement
    • Works around your schedule — say no anytime

    Volunteer — what you can expect

    About four hours a month keeps a shift covered. Here's what those hours actually look like.

    Onboarding

    An orientation night every month covers handling, cleaning protocols, and what a shift actually looks like. No cat-handling experience needed — just reliability.

    Lots of fun — and lots of pets

    Adoption-day setup, transport runs between fosters and the vet, photography, and events. Every role comes with more kitten time than you're prepared for.

    Building community

    You'll join a group chat of fosters and volunteers who celebrate every adoption together. Students welcome — we sign off volunteer hours.

    Volunteer experiences

    Maya, 29

    My first litter was two bottle babies who screamed all night. Eight weeks later I handed them to their new family and cried in the parking lot — then signed up for the next litter that evening. Fostering is the best worst idea I've ever had.

    Priya, 34

    I started as an adoption-day volunteer because I couldn't have cats in my rental. Two years and one lease change later, I've fostered eleven cats and failed exactly once — she's asleep on my couch right now.

    Dan, 44

    I do the vet runs. It's an hour a week of driving with a meowing passenger, and it's somehow the calmest part of my schedule. My kids keep score of how many cats Dad has chauffeured — we're at 63.

    Our Wishlist

    Prefer to give supplies instead of money? These are the things we run out of fastest. New or gently used, everything goes straight into foster homes — bring donations to any adoption event, or message us to arrange a pickup.

    Food Items

    • Kitten wet food (pâté — young kittens can't crunch yet)
    • Kitten formula (KMR) for bottle babies
    • Kitten dry food for the older litters
    • Lickable treats for socializing shy cats

    Toys and Enrichment

    • Wand toys and crinkle balls
    • Scratching posts and cardboard scratchers
    • Cat trees (gently used is perfect)
    • Puzzle feeders for the clever ones

    Grooming and Care

    • Unscented wipes and cotton pads
    • Flea combs and soft brushes
    • Nail clippers (kitten size)
    • Kitten-safe shampoo

    Daily Needs

    • Clumping litter and low-sided litter boxes
    • Fleece blankets and washable bedding
    • Heating pads for orphaned newborns
    • Carriers for vet runs and adoption days

    Technology Donations

    • Digital kitchen scales — daily weigh-ins catch problems early
    • Baby monitors or spare webcams for foster rooms
    • Pet-store gift cards for whatever the week brings

    Please skip open food bags and scented litter — sensitive kitten noses say thank you.
